#Topics covered: Elements of infosec, risk management and info assurance.

Orgs spend lots of money and time on defenses and mitigations against attacks. There are some fundamental concepts that go into the planning and
implementation of these defenses.

Alongside knowing what you are protecting against, I need to know what I'm protecting. Performing risk assessments will help with that. Identifying
potential risks and associated potential losses can help with decision making around where to expend limited resources -- budget, staffing, capital
expenditures, and so on. It'll also help security professionals determine what policies they need in place to guide the actions of employees overall
and, more specifically, info tech staff. From policies flow standards and procedures. They are all considered essential for determining how a company
approaches protecting its information assets.

Once I have my policies in place, decisions can be made about how best to protect information resources. Beyond staffing, there is technology that can
be brought to bear.

The technology alone doesn't provide protection. Where these elements and devices are placed is important, and those decisions are made based on ideas
such as defense in depth and defense in breadth.

#The CIA triad

The triad is a set of three attributes, or properties, that define what security is. The elements are confidentiality, integrity and availability. Each of
these properties needs to be taken into consideration when developing security plans. Each of them, to varying degrees of importance, will be essential in
planning defenses. Not every defense will incorporate each of the three properties to the same extent. Some defenses impact only one property, for instance.
From an attack perspective, a tester or adversary would be looking to compromise one of these elements within an organization. The properties all have equal
value when it comes to the overall security posture of an organization. If any of the properties is removed or compromised, the security of my org has also
been compromised. It takes all these properties to ensure that me and my informaton are being protected.

##Confidentiality

Confidentiality means keeping secrets. This encompasses a lot of different aspects. It means making sure no one gets unauthorized access to information. This
may mean using strong passwords on my user accounts to make sure attackers can't get in. It may mean keeping certain information offline so it can't be access
remotely. Commonly, though, a simple way to achieve confidentiality is through the use of encryption.

When I talk about confidentiality, I should be thinking about it in two dimensions - static and dynamic. Static would be protecting data that is considered "at
rest", which means is not moving. The dynamic is when the data is moving or "in motion". This refers to data when it is being sent from one place to another. As
the data is being transmitted, it is in motion. It's this transmission that makes it dynamic and not necessarily that is being altered, though data being sent
from one place to another could definitely be experiencing alteration through interaction with the user and the application.

When I'm making use of encryption for web based communication, the Secure Sockets Layer/Transport Layer Security (SSL/TLS) protocols are used. While TLS has long
since superseded SSL, it is still sometimes referred to as SSL/TLS. Regardless of how it's referred to, though, it is a set of mechanisms for encryption data. SSL
and TLS both specify how to generate encryption keys from data that is known, as well as some partial data that is transmitted from one side to the other.

Since encrypted data can't be read without a key, the confidentiality of the data is protected. 

##Integrity

A man-in-the-middle attack is one way for an attacker to compromise integrity. The attacker intercepts the data in transit, alters it and sends it on the way. When I
browse to a website that uses encryption, a certificate is used to provide keying, meaning the certificate holds keys used for encryption and decryption. When a certificate
contains a name that is different from the hostname being visited, my browser will generate an error.

A certificate might show that it belongs to site.com. However, the website that was visited was etis.com.br. If I did not know that one website belongs to the other, I might
begin to question whether I was being hijacked in some way. An attack in this situation may have resulted in an attacker gathering information from my session, which I
wouldn't have expected because I believed the session was encrypted. Worse, the information I got could have been altered while the attacker got the real information.

Note: Uma sessao nada mais eh do que identificacao do visitante. Entao, quando eu acesso um site, talvez ele defina uma "sessao" para mim, ou seja uma identificacao. Em
determinado momento, eu faco login. Essa informacao sera atualizada na sessao que me identifica no servidor.

Integrity, it turns out, is very complex.

#Information Assurance and Risk

Risk is the intersection of loss and probability. Probabilities of information security events are hard to calculate. Loss is easier to quantify.
$risk = probability * loss.

It can be tempting to think about risk as loss. Predicting every possible event can be challenging. This is what makes information security difficult --
understanding events, probabilities and outcomes for my org and then planning to protect against negative events.

A threat is something that has the possibility to incur a breach of confidentiality, integrity or availability. The avenue this breach may take is called a vulnerability.
A vulnerability is a weakness in a system. This may be its software, its configuration, or how the entire information solution is put together. When the vulnerability is
triggered, it is called an exploit. We exploit vulnerabilities, though not all vulnerabilities can be exploited. Race conditions are examples of vulnerabilities that may
not be able to be exploited.

A threat agent or a threat actor is an entity, like a person or grooup, that can instantiate a threat. The threat agent is who manifests a threat. The pathway the
threat agent takes to exploit a vulnerability is called a threat vector. All of these concepts are important because they help to better understand where risk lies.
Once I have identified what resources I care about, I should think about what threat agents may care about those resources. This can help me to identify potential
vulnerabilities. Once I know what my vulnerabilities are and the potential threat vectors, I can start to think about how I am going to protect against those threats.

Information assurance is the practice of identifying risks associated with the information assets, then instituting controls to better protect the asset or
resource. A control is a means of ensuring the confidentiality, integrity or availability of an information security resource. These controls may be preventive, detective
or corrective. A preventive control is one that intends to keep something bad from happening. Unfortunately, ths is not always possible, which means
detective controls are useful. A detective control is one where a violation of confidentiality, integrity or availability is identified so the violation can
be addressed. Finally, a corrective control is one that intends to limit the amount of damage that may have been confused.

Security controls end up having two factors. In addition to controls having goals, as described earlier, they will fall into one of three different types. A control
may be technical, administrative or physical. A technical control is hardware or software that has been implemented to provide protection of assets. This may be an
authentication solution, a firewall, antivirus or a security incident and event manager used to alert on something bad happening. An administrative control is
is a policy, procedure, or guideline that gives direction on how protections should be implemented. Finally, physical controls are used to keep people away from
assets in a kinetic way, meaning someone can't walk up to a building and just enter it.

Information assurance is about managing risk. This means not just identifying risk and how to categorize it, but also making business decisions about how to handle the risk.
Businesses may handle risk in different ways, depending on the level of risk and their risk appetite.

- Acceptance: It's an informed process where the business fully understands the nature of the risk, meaning the impact and the probability and choose not to do anything
about the risk. This means if the risk is actualized , the business will be responsible for all the losses and costs associated with it, as well as any other damage that
may result. This approach means nothing else is done to the circumstances related to the risk identified.

- Transference: The business may choose to transfer the risk to another entity to manage in some way. One common way to implement risk transference is to buy insurance.

- Mitigation: Risk mitigation is implementing controls that will reduce the risk. Risk reduction can occur on the two vectors that constitute risk -- loss and probability.

- Avoidance: Business may choose to not engage in the activity that could introduce risk to begin with.

#Policies, Standards and Procedures

Security is a business enabler, not a business driver. Because security is a business enabler, the business sets the parameters around what is important and the means
to protect what is important. It does that by creating policies. Once the policies are created, standards are built out of those policies. Closest to where the
work actually gets done are procedures. These are developed because of what the standards say.

##Security Policies

A security policy is a statement of intention with regard to the resources of a business. It defines what a company considers to be security -- what resources need to be
protected, how resources should be utilized in a proper manner, how resources can or should be accessed. These policies are essential to good corporate governance,
since the policies are lines that management draws. This means that having management set the tone and direction is not only a good idea, it's also required.
Management and the board of directors have an obligation to the stakeholders in the business -- the individual owners or the shareholders.

Security policies are not only about defining important resources, they are also about setting expectations of employees. Many organizations, for example, have
an acceptable use policy. This is about defining what users can and cannot do.

Keep in mind as I'm thinking about security policy that the goals of my policies should be the confidentiality, integrity and availability of information resources.
All information resources should be confidential, have integrity and be available within the parameters defined by the business. Different information assets will have
different levels of confidentiality, and not all information has to be available all the time. This is also part of what security policy is for -- to help
classify and prioritize the information assets of the org.

Not all information resources are bits and bytes stored on computer systems. One area that policy should always take into consideration is how the human resources
are to be handled.

Policies should be revisited on a regular basis. They are sufficiently high-level that they shouldn't change every time there is a new set of technologies available,
but they should evolve with changes in the threat landscape. As information resources change and threat agents change, the policies may need to adapt to be
responsive to those changes. As this is an area of corporate governance, any policy change would have to be approved by whatever management structure is in place --
business owners; board of directors and so on.

Keep in mind that security policies are all high-level. They don't provide specifics, such as how the policies should be implemented. If I'm looking at security policies
and I'm starting to think about the operational impacts and how the admiistrative would handle the policy, I'm too close to the ground, and it's time to beat my wings
a bit more to get much higher up. Also, I'm thinking conceptually for what should be long-term rather than something specific to a point in time. This means that technology
and solutions have no place in the policy. Other information security program elements will take care of those aspects.

##Security Standards

The security policy is at the top of the food chain. There may also be subpolicies that flow down from the top-level security policies. The subpolicy should
refer to the overall policy so the high-level policy doesn't get lost. Below the policy level, though, are security standards. A standard is direction about how policies
should be implemented. The standard starts down the path of how we get from statements of intent to implementation, so I start to drill down from the high level of the
policy.

There are two meanings for the term security standard.

There are sets of standards that provide guidance for organizations and are managed by standards bodies. The NIST has a set of standards, documented in several special
publications. The ISO maintains ISO 27001 and ISO 27002. There are other standards documents that may be relevant to me, depending where I'm in the world.

The second type of standard is one that provides detailed guidance for how to implement policies within orgs.
