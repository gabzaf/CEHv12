Ethical hacking or Penetration testing.

To keep myself viable as a professional I need to behave and perform my work in an ethical manner. As part of the code of ethics, I will be sworn
to keep information I obtain as part of my work private, paying particular attention to protecting the information and intelectual property of
employers and clients. When I am attacking systems that belong to other people, I could be provided with internal information that is sensitive.
I could also come across some critical information vital to the organization for which I am working. Failing to protect any of that data violates
the code of ethics by compromising the confidentiality of that information.

I am expected to disclose information that needs to be disclosed to the people who have engaged my services. This includes any issues that I have
identified. I am also expected to disclose potential conflicts of interest that I may have. It's important to be transparent in my dealings and
also do the right thing when it comes to protecting my clients, employers and their business interests. Additionally, if I come across something
that could have an impact on a large number of people across the Internet, I am expected to disclose it in a responsible manner.

There will be times when the testing I am performing may cause damage to a service provided by the infrastructure of the company I am working for or
with. As long as this is unintentional or agreed to be acceptable by the company, this is okay. One way to alleviate this concern is to keep lines
of communications open at all times. If it happens that an unexpected outage occurs, ensuring that the right people know so it can be remedies is
essential.

When I am taking on a new engagement, it's essential to be clear about the expectations for my services. If I have the scope in writing,
everyting is clear and documented.

#Overview of Ethical Hacking

There is an urgent need to improve how information security is handled. It's believed that to protect against attacks, I have to be able to
understand those attacks. Ideally, I need to replicate the attacks. If businesses are testing attacks against their own infrastructure early and
often, those businesses could be in a better position to improve their defenses and keep the real attackers out.

This type os testing is what ethical hacking really is. It's all about ferreting out problems wth the goal of improving the overall security
posture of the target. This may be for a company in terms of their infrastructure or even desktop systems. It may also be performing testing
against software to identify bugs that can be used to compromise the software and, subsequently, the system where the software is running.

Ethical hacking can be done under many different names. I may not always see the term ethical hacking, especially when I am looking at the job
titles. I may see the term penetration testing or red teaming, which is generally considered a specific type of pen test where the testers are
adversarial to the organization and network under test. A red teamer would actually act like an attacker, meaning they would try to be stealthy
so as not to be detected.

One of the challeging aspects of this sort of activity is having to think like an attacker. Testing of this nature is often challenging and
requires a different way of thinking. When doing any sort of testing, including ethical hacking, a methodology is important, as it helps ensure
that my actions are both repeatable and verifiable. 

#Attack Modeling

Using a methodology is valuable when it comes to ethical hacking or security testing. Methodology can help with consistency, repeatability and
process improvement.

There are some testing or assessment methodologies that get used throughout the industry, including the Penetration Testing Execution Standard
(PTES) and the Open Source Security Testing Methodology Manual (OSSTMM). These methodologies are typically built around expectations of what an
attacker would do or how attackers operate. These may not map perfectly to how attackers operate in the real world, but they do help to ensure
a consistency and breadth of approach to security testing, which makes them valuable. In addition, many common security testing methodologies are
models of how attackers operate. The first is the cyber kill chain, another is the attack life cycle, while a third is the MITRE ATT&CK framework.
